I am a physical chemist and R&D engineer whose work has included equipment development for high-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) applications. Alongside this professional work, I pursue independent projects in software development, structured data, scientific analysis, and applications of multimodal generative AI to industrial-design-driven product engineering.

My software work explores how adaptable data and AI infrastructure can support flexible research workflows and privacy-sensitive applications. I am particularly interested in combining rapid AI-assisted development of task-specific transformations with mature, inspectable tools, and in hybrid architectures that use local or cloud models according to capability, privacy, regulatory, and operational requirements.

I am also actively exploring the limits of frontier cloud models to complex research and engineering projects. One such project followed development of early frontier multimodal reasoning LLMs and examined their capabilities and limitations through structured, skeptical analysis of scientific manuscripts. It covered methodology, experimental figures, physical and chemical plausibility, internal consistency, and semantic and linguistic issues, and produced three arXiv preprints.

SQLite stands out as a serverless, in-process relational engine for these workflows. Data or metadata exported from less accessible formats can be explored and transformed using its mature ecosystem of graphical tools and SQL, while LanceDB offers a promising local counterpart for vector search. Applying such systems beyond conventional use cases benefits from understanding their APIs, extension architectures, and build processes rather than treating them as black boxes. My public projects include pure-SQL hierarchy management, object-oriented SQLite access from VBA, custom builds and extensions, compact binary vector storage, regular-expression match extraction, and direct C API testing with Pytest and CFFI. I document this work and other technical investigations on GitHub.
