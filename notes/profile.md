I am a physical chemist and R&D engineer whose work has included equipment development for high-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) applications. Alongside this professional work, I pursue independent projects in software development, structured data, scientific analysis, and applications of multimodal generative AI to industrial-design-driven product engineering.

A recurring interest in these independent projects is how adaptable data and AI infrastructure can support flexible research workflows and privacy-sensitive applications. I am particularly interested in using AI to accelerate the development of task-specific transformations built on mature, inspectable tools. I also explore hybrid architectures that distribute work between local and cloud models according to task needs, model capabilities, data sensitivity, regulatory constraints, and operational considerations.

My work with frontier cloud models examines their capabilities and limitations in complex research and engineering contexts, including a sustained investigation of early multimodal reasoning models through structured, skeptical analysis of scientific manuscripts. The analyses covered methodology, experimental figures, physical and chemical plausibility, internal consistency, and semantic and linguistic issues, and were documented in three arXiv preprints.

Another direction explores local data systems as adaptable foundations for both unconventional R&D workflows and purpose-built information systems. SQLite is particularly useful as a foundation because it combines a robust embedded relational database engine and SQL interface with a mature ecosystem of graphical database tools. This combination enables convenient exploration and transformation of semi-structured data and metadata exported from less accessible formats. Within R&D workflows, AI-assisted development makes it practical to build export, import, and transformation pipelines and focused tools tailored to particular tasks. Augmented by local vector-search systems such as LanceDB and rapidly improving local LLMs, the same infrastructure can underpin local-first applications for document intelligence and knowledge retrieval. I document the publicly shareable parts of this work and other technical investigations on GitHub.
