I am a physical chemist and R&D engineer. My engineering work has included equipment development for high-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) applications.

In independent projects, I explore software development, structured data, scientific analysis, and applications of multimodal generative AI to industrial-design-driven product engineering.

One multimodal LLM research project examined model capabilities and limitations through structured, skeptical analysis of scientific manuscripts. It covered methodology, experimental figures, physical and chemical plausibility, internal consistency, and semantic and linguistic issues, and produced three arXiv preprints.

My software projects explore how adaptable data infrastructure—including relational and vector databases, mature GUI and SQL tools, and locally deployable AI—can support flexible research workflows. I am also interested in hybrid architectures that use cloud models selectively when their capabilities justify the associated trade-offs.

One direction combines rapid AI-assisted development of task-specific bulk data transformations with stable, inspectable infrastructure. Data or metadata exported from less accessible formats, for example, can be explored and analyzed in SQLite using mature database tools. SQLite is particularly useful as a serverless, in-process relational engine; I am also investigating LanceDB as a local vector-search counterpart.

Using such systems beyond conventional workflows benefits from understanding their APIs, extension architectures, and build processes rather than treating them as black boxes. This enables focused features that reuse established infrastructure instead of replacing it. My public projects include pure-SQL hierarchy management, object-oriented SQLite access from VBA, custom Windows builds and statically integrated extensions, compact binary vector storage, regular-expression functions returning match arrays, and direct C API testing with Pytest and CFFI.

At the application level, I explore how local, cloud, and hybrid AI workflows can be matched to particular tasks. Local execution offers privacy, control, and offline operation where cloud services may be restricted or unsuitable; cloud models may offer greater capability or lower operational overhead where their use is appropriate. Hybrid workflows can combine these advantages—for example, by keeping sensitive data and core processing local while using cloud models selectively for suitable tasks. I document these projects and other technical investigations on GitHub.
