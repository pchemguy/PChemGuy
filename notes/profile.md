I am a physical chemist and R&D engineer whose work has included equipment development for high-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) applications. Alongside this professional work, I pursue independent projects in software development, structured data, scientific analysis, and applications of multimodal generative AI to industrial-design-driven product engineering.

Across these projects, I explore how adaptable data and AI infrastructure can support flexible research workflows and privacy-sensitive applications. I am particularly interested in combining rapid AI-assisted development of task-specific transformations with mature, inspectable tools, and in hybrid architectures that use local or cloud models according to capability, privacy, regulatory, and operational requirements.

One direction investigates how frontier cloud models perform on complex research and engineering tasks. A project in this direction tracked the early development of multimodal reasoning models by applying them to structured, skeptical analysis of scientific manuscripts. The work examined methodology, experimental figures, physical and chemical plausibility, internal consistency, and semantic and linguistic issues, and was documented in three arXiv preprints.

Another direction explores local data systems as adaptable foundations for unconventional R&D workflows. SQLite, a robust embedded relational database engine with a mature ecosystem of graphical tools and SQL, can bring semi-structured data and metadata from less accessible formats into an environment suited to interactive exploration and transformation. AI-assisted development can accelerate the creation of task-specific export, import, and transformation pipelines, as well as focused tools for working with the resulting data. Augmented by local vector-search systems such as LanceDB and rapidly improving local LLMs, this infrastructure can underpin local-first tools for document intelligence, knowledge retrieval, and research analysis. I document the publicly shareable parts of this work and other technical investigations on GitHub.
