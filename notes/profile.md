I am a physical chemist and R&D engineer whose work has included equipment development for high-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) applications. Alongside this professional work, I pursue independent projects in software development, structured data, scientific analysis, and applications of multimodal generative AI to industrial-design-driven product engineering.

My software work explores how adaptable data and AI infrastructure can support flexible research workflows and privacy-sensitive applications. I am particularly interested in combining rapid AI-assisted development of task-specific transformations with mature, inspectable tools, and in hybrid architectures that use local or cloud models according to capability, privacy, regulatory, and operational requirements.

One direction actively explores the capabilities and limits of frontier cloud models in complex research and engineering projects. One such project followed the early development of frontier multimodal reasoning models through structured, skeptical analysis of scientific manuscripts. The analysis covered methodology, experimental figures, physical and chemical plausibility, internal consistency, and semantic and linguistic issues, and resulted in three arXiv preprints.

Another direction explores local data systems as adaptable foundations for unconventional R&D workflows. For example, the robust embeddable RDBMS engine SQLite with its mature ecosystem of graphical database tools and SQL may be particularly useful for interactive exploration of semi-structured data provided in less accessible formats. Importantly, modern AI enables rapid development of data and metadata export/import/transform pipelines tailored to specific tasks and establish focused optimized tooling. When combined with complementary local foundation for vector search, such as LanceDB and rapidly evolving local LLM models, these components form the basis for prospective local data and information intelligence applications. I document the open part of this work and other technical investigations on GitHub.
